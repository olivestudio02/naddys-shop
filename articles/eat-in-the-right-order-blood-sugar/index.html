Eat in the Right Order: How Carbs Last Can Lower Your Sugar Spike

By Nadeem · 21 November 2026

Quick noteThis is general information from my own research and experience, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before changing your diet, starting a supplement or a new exercise plan, especially if you have a health condition or take medicines.

Two people eat the same lunch: the same rice, chicken and salad, with the same calories. One gets a big sugar spike and a crash an hour later. The other barely spikes. The difference can be the order they ate it in.

What the research found

In a study from Weill Cornell Medicine, 16 adults with type 2 diabetes ate the same meal on three different days: carbs first, carbs last, or carbs split in the middle. When carbs came last, the blood sugar peak was about 54% lower than with carbs first, and about 25% less insulin was needed. Those volunteers had type 2 diabetes, where the effect is strongest. Later studies in prediabetes and in healthy young adults saw the same direction, though the effect is smaller when blood sugar is already healthy.

What a sugar spike is

Carbs break down into glucose, which enters your blood. Your pancreas releases insulin to clear it. A small rise is normal. A big spike means a big wave of insulin, and while insulin is high, fat burning is switched off. The goal is not zero carbs. It is a gentle hill instead of a mountain.

Two built-in brakes

The fibre net. Vegetables eaten first bring fibre that forms a gel in your gut, so the sugar from your carbs soaks through slowly. Fibre also takes up room, so you tend to eat a little less rice.

Protein and GLP-1. Protein triggers your gut to release GLP-1, a hormone that slows the stomach's exit gate and tells your brain you are getting full. A Japanese study found that fish or meat before rice slowed stomach emptying, raised GLP-1, and lowered the sugar rise after the meal.

The crash that makes you snack

What goes up fast can come down fast. After a big spike, blood sugar can dip below where it started two or three hours later, which makes you sleepy and pulls you towards biscuits and sugary coffee. In the large PREDICT study, people with the biggest dips felt hungry sooner and ate around 300 more calories over the day.

How I build every plate

  1. Vegetables first. A salad, a clear vegetable soup, or the vegetables on your plate.
  2. Protein next. Chicken, fish or eggs. Vegetarian options: paneer, tofu, soya chunks, Greek yogurt or chickpeas.
  3. Carbs last. Rice, roti, bread or pasta. You do not need a timer. Just eat slowly.

At home, carbs usually come mixed with a curry or side dish. An easy fix: eat about 70% of your protein first and keep the last 30% to enjoy with your rice or roti. The meal still tastes like home.

Honest limits

This does not make carbs free. A mountain of rice in the right order is still a mountain of rice. Fat loss still needs a modest calorie deficit, and the easiest route is building meals around protein and vegetables.

Habits that help the order work

  • Move after the meal: a 10-minute walk, or three sets of 15 bodyweight squats. Even 2 to 5 minutes of light movement softens the spike.
  • Lift weights four or more days a week. Muscle is your biggest storage tank for sugar.
  • Aim for 8,000 to 10,000 steps a day.
  • Skip sugary drinks and watch hidden sugar in tea, biscuits, packaged snacks, cereals and sauces.
  • Eat dinner early, three to four hours before sleep. Even one short night can make your body handle the next breakfast worse.
