How to Choose a Whey Protein (Without Getting Fooled)

By Naddy · 26 November 2026

Quick noteThis is general information from my own research and experience, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor before starting a new supplement, especially if you have a health condition or take medicines.

Walk into any supplement aisle and every tub claims to be the best. Most of the difference comes down to four things on the label. Here is how I check them.

First: do you even need whey?

Whey is just a convenient protein source. If your meals already hit your protein target, you do not need it. It earns its place when food falls short, like busy workdays or after training.

Concentrate vs isolate vs blend

  • Concentrate: the most common and usually cheapest. It has a little more lactose and fat, so it can bother sensitive stomachs.
  • Isolate: filtered further, so it is lower in lactose and often lighter on the stomach. Costs more per tub.
  • Blend: a mix. Check the order of ingredients: whey protein should come first.

The four label checks

1. Protein per scoop

Divide the grams of protein by the scoop size. Around 24 to 27 g protein in a 30 to 33 g scoop is a healthy range. Much lower means more filler.

2. The ingredient list

Whey protein should be the first ingredient. Be careful with long lists of thickeners, sweeteners and "proprietary blends" that hide what is actually inside.

3. Third-party testing

Look for lab test reports or authenticity details on the brand's page. Reports are the simplest way to see that the protein number on the tub is real.

4. Real seller, real batch

Fake tubs are a genuine problem online. Buy from the brand's official store or a seller Amazon itself lists as authorised, check the seal and the batch and expiry details when it arrives.

Price per gram of protein

The cheapest tub is not always the cheapest protein. Divide the price by total grams of protein in the tub. That one number makes brands easy to compare.

If whey upsets your stomach

Try an isolate, mix with water instead of milk, and start with a half scoop. If it still bothers you, a lactose-free option or food protein is fine too.

The short version

Pick a whey where protein is first on the label, the protein per scoop is solid, the brand shows testing, and you buy from a real seller. Match isolate or concentrate to your stomach and budget, then stop overthinking it and train.
