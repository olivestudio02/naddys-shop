Intermittent Fasting: Does It Work? The Simple 12-Hour Rule

Does intermittent fasting really burn fat? The studies say it is not magic. But there is a simple way to use it that works for me, and I will share it here.

What it is

Intermittent fasting means eating only inside a set window of the day. The most popular version is 16:8: sixteen hours without food and all meals in the other eight. For example, you eat between noon and 8 p.m. and nothing from 8 p.m. until noon the next day.

What the research says

  • A 2022 New England Journal of Medicine trial of 139 adults for one year compared calorie restriction with time-restricted eating against calorie restriction alone. Weight loss was about 8 kg versus 6.3 kg, and the difference was not statistically significant.
  • A 2020 JAMA Internal Medicine trial of 116 adults compared 12 weeks of 16:8 with normal meals and found almost no difference in weight.
  • A Cochrane review of 22 studies with nearly 2,000 people found little to no difference in weight loss between intermittent fasting and traditional dietary advice.

That does not mean fasting is bad. It means fasting is not magic. It is another way to eat fewer calories, and weight loss still comes down to your weekly intake. The best plan is the one you can stick to.

Step 1: the 12 to 14 hour default

Finish dinner by 7 p.m. and have your first meal at 7 or 8 the next morning. That is already 12 to 14 hours, and it is basically a normal night's sleep with the kitchen closed. It cuts late-night snacking, where many hidden calories live, with no hunger games. For most people this is enough.

Step 2: my 16-hour rest days

This is my own routine, not a rule from a study. I have done it for months and I see results. On days I lift, I keep the 12 to 14 hour window, because my muscles need fuel. On two non-lifting days a week I stretch the window to 16 hours, since I need fewer calories on lower-activity days. In the extra hours I do cardio on an empty stomach: a medium-pace walk, plus a few ab exercises some days. Choose any cardio you enjoy, but a medium-pace walk is a good default. Then I break my fast around 12 or 1 with a proper protein meal.

A fair caveat: studies comparing fasted and fed cardio over a few weeks tend to find similar total fat loss. It works for me because it is simple, I can repeat it every week, and consistency is what decides results. Inside the fast I keep it clean: water, warm lime water or green tea, nothing sugary, and no alcohol.

Guardrails

  • Do not fast longer than feels good on lifting days.
  • Protein at every meal, and lift four to five days a week.
  • Aim for 8,000 to 10,000 steps, split into short walks after meals, and keep a fixed bedtime.
  • If you feel dizzy during fasted cardio, stop and eat.

Sources

  • Liu et al., New England Journal of Medicine, 2022.
  • Lowe et al., JAMA Internal Medicine, 2020.
  • Cochrane review CD015610 (intermittent fasting for weight loss).
